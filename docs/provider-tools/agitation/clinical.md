# Agitation SafeSteps clinical specification

## Intended population

Children and adolescents with escalating agitation in an emergency department,
including neurodivergent patients and patients with communication differences.

## Clinical purpose

Prompt environmental modification, caregiver-informed communication,
cause-directed de-escalation, optional reviewed risk screening, oral-first
options when appropriate, interaction checks, restraint safety, and timed
reassessment.

## Evidence baseline

- AAP Pediatrics BRACHA-S emergency-department study, 2025.
- Additional de-escalation, medication, restraint, and neurodiversity guidance
  must be selected and versioned before implementation.

## Explicit non-goals

- No psychiatric diagnosis, involuntary-treatment decision, automatic risk
  label, medication selection, restraint order, or security instruction.
- No unreviewed BRACHA-S calculation or universal medication pathway.
- No behavioral notes, identifiers, or persistent risk score.

## Questions requiring clinical resolution

- Which rapid observations are valid outside the BRACHA-S study setting?
- How are pain, hypoxia, delirium, toxidrome, trauma, communication needs, and
  medication adverse effects surfaced without diagnosing?
- Which drug interactions and monitoring requirements must be shown together?
- What reassessment and restraint-safety language is legally and locally valid?

## Required named reviewers

PEM, child and adolescent psychiatry, developmental-behavioral pediatrics,
pediatric pharmacy, emergency nursing, child-life or behavioral health,
security and restraint leadership, institutional legal/compliance, and
regulatory review.

## Simulation acceptance cases

- An autistic child with sensory overload surfaces environmental actions before
  medication content.
- A hypoxic child case keeps medical-cause assessment prominent.
- An interaction case cannot display two incompatible options without a warning.
- A restraint case exposes monitoring and release criteria within two actions.

## Release boundary

Remain Planned until evidence selection, score licensing and validation,
medication review, legal review, representative-family input, and simulation are
complete.
